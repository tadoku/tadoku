Tadoku design discussion · P0.2 · Study 4

Straight rails, firmer controls, and a mark of our own.

This pass removes the triangular border join, tests a 2px lower edge only on interactive components, and explores five compact Tadoku logo ideas without borrowing the current K-shaped language.

01 · Border construction

One uninterrupted accent rail

The triangle was a mitered join: the browser was blending a thick left border into thin top and bottom borders. Here, the violet rail is an independent positioned strip that covers both endpoints, so its full six-pixel edge stays perfectly straight.

Less nesting, clearer hierarchy

The rail belongs only to the outermost meaningful container. Inside it, content groups use whitespace and horizontal separators instead of more boxes. This preserves the bookplate character without producing frames inside frames.

02 · Interaction weight

A 2px lower edge, where it earns its keep

The extra pixel makes controls feel deliberate and tactile. It is restricted to elements people can press or type into; content containers keep a quiet 1px outline.

03 · Logo territories

Five marks that can belong to Tadoku

These are deliberately schematic, code-native sketches for choosing a concept—not finished logos. Each is shown at favicon size to expose which ideas survive when small. The violet remains a structural accent, not decoration added afterward.

1 · Open Book T

An open book built around a violet T-shaped spine. Directly signals reading while giving the name an initial.

Fit: most literal, editorial, slightly formal.
Shortlist

2 · Bookmark T

A bold T interrupted by a bookmark ribbon. Simple enough to own, with a clear reading cue at small sizes.

Fit: strongest compact app icon; traditional but not nostalgic cosplay.

3 · Library Shelf

A run of book spines with one violet volume leaning forward. It conveys collection, progress, and personal reading history.

Fit: warmest library cue; can become busy at 16px.
Shortlist

4 · Reading Loop

A continuous page-turn path: read, record, continue. More abstract and ownable than a conventional book icon.

Fit: most distinctive and active; needs refinement to avoid an infinity-app feel.

5 · Catalog Card

A library catalog card whose violet rail mirrors the interface. It connects the product identity directly to the design system.

Fit: best system connection; risks reading as a generic document icon.
