Tadoku design discussion · P0.2 · Study 6

Language immersion, not a letter-shaped logo.

A third logo reset based on what Tadoku actually does: build a habit of consuming foreign-language media through reading, listening, tracking, and friendly contests.

01 · Dark primary action

Light text, with a darker action violet

Light text does read more naturally on a filled primary button. In dark mode, the structural accent can stay bright while the button fill uses a deeper companion violet, preserving contrast instead of placing white text on pale lavender.

Separate accent ink from action fill

One violet does not have to perform every job. Bright ink violet remains excellent for rails, focus rings, and small marks in dark mode; a deeper violet makes a better button surface.

  • Dark structural accent: lifted violet for thin lines and small details.
  • Dark action fill: deeper violet behind light text.
  • Hover: a controlled lift in brightness, without changing the text color.

02 · Brand territories

Five concepts based on immersion and momentum

These marks are evaluated beside a neutral Tadoku wordmark and at favicon size. None contains a hidden initial. They deliberately range from broad brand ideas to narrower contest and media ideas so we can decide what the identity should emphasize.

Core promise

Understand more by steadily consuming native-language material.

Supported modes

Reading, listening, video, and other trackable media.

Social energy

Friendly contests create momentum, but winning is not the only point.

Brand judgment

Keep the master mark broad; let contests have their own badge.

Immersion Field and Language Weave are the broadest master-brand territories. Living Signal is viable if listening should become the strongest association. Friendly Pace and Media Feed are useful tests, but feel better suited to a contest badge or product feature than the permanent Tadoku mark.
