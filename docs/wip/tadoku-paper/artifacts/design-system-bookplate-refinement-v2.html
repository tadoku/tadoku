Bookplate refinement · second study

One border language. One annotation color.

This pass removes the competing outlines from the first preview. Every boundary now comes from one neutral ink family; thickness communicates role, and color is reserved for meaning.

Working recommendation: keep Bookplate, simplify it. Preserve the current wordmark, shift the accent toward a darker ink violet, and treat any “library plate” treatment as an optional lockup—not as a replacement logo.
01 / Borders

A four-role border grammar

These are the only border treatments available to components. Statuses no longer invent their own outlines, and violet is never a normal border color.

Role 01

Structural

Major shells, navigation boundaries, and dialog edges.

1px · ink neutral
Role 02

Component

Fields, cards, rows, tags, and internal separation.

1px · warm neutral
Role 03

Interactive

Buttons and fields gain tactile weight only underneath.

1px + 2px lower rule
Role 04

Floating

Menus and dialogs use structural borders plus hard depth.

1px + 3px offset

03 / Logo

Refine the setting, not the wordmark.

The existing geometric wordmark already suits the sharp system. Its angled violet wedge is useful: it reads like a page marker. The lowest-risk improvement is to unify that wedge with the chosen annotation ink and establish intentional lockups.
